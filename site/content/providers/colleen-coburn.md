Colleen Coburn has practiced medicine for more than three decades: in Coast
Guard clinics, in pediatric trauma bays, and in operating rooms across South
Florida.

She began in 1995 as a U.S. Coast Guard Health Services Technician, rising to
Clinic Supervisor before earning her Master of Science in Physician Assistant
Sciences through the University of Nebraska's Interservice Physician Assistant
Program. As a commissioned officer she served as Senior Medical Officer at USCG
Base Miami Beach, the primary care provider for more than 1,500 active-duty and
reserve personnel, responsible for everything from routine medicine and minor
surgery to emergency stabilization, behavioral health, and substance-use
evaluation. In 2019 she was one of only two candidates nationwide interviewed
for Tactical Medical Officer with the White House Medical Unit. She retired in
2020 as a Lieutenant, after 25 years of military service.

Her surgical career has run alongside it. At Nicklaus Children's Hospital she
served as a Pediatric Surgery Physician Assistant, stabilizing arriving trauma
patients and first-assisting in the operating room. At the Paley Orthopedic &
Spine Institute she was the lead orthopedic trauma provider at St. Mary's
Medical Center. She continues to operate as a surgical first assistant at
hospitals throughout Broward County, across nine specialties: orthopedic,
trauma, general, plastic, breast oncology, neurosurgery, ENT, gynecologic, and
laparoscopic.

That range is unusual, and it is the point. Colleen has managed a patient's
chronic conditions in clinic, stabilized them in an emergency, and stood across
the operating table from them. Very few primary care providers have seen the
whole arc, and it is what allows her to tell you, plainly, what a proposed
procedure actually involves and whether you need it.

She is board-certified by the NCCPA and licensed in Florida with full
prescribing authority. She lives in Hollywood with her husband and children.
